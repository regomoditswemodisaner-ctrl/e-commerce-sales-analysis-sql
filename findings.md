# E-Commerce Sales Analysis — Findings \& Recommendations

## Overall Sales Performance

The fictional dataset contains 20 orders and 41 units sold.

Total revenue was $1,100, with an average order value of $55.

## Product Category Performance

Electronics generated the highest revenue at $600.

The remaining categories generated:

* Sports: $220
* Stationery: $134
* Beauty: $86
* Home \& Kitchen: $60

This indicates that revenue was concentrated more heavily in the Electronics category within this dataset.

## Customer Analysis

Customer spending was divided into three segments:

* High Value: 10 customers
* Medium Value: 8 customers
* Low Value: 2 customers

The segmentation provides a simple framework for identifying customers who may have different purchasing patterns and may require different engagement strategies.

## Product Performance

Product-level analysis was used to identify products generating higher and lower levels of revenue and units sold.

Lower-revenue products were identified using a revenue threshold of $50.

These products could be investigated further to understand whether lower sales are related to demand, pricing, product positioning, or limited orders.

## Time-Based Analysis

Monthly and quarterly analysis was used to examine changes in:

* Revenue
* Number of orders
* Units sold
* Average order value

These measures can help a business monitor changes in sales activity over time.

## Customer Retention

Customer purchase activity was analyzed using each customer's most recent order date.

Customers were classified as:

* Active — purchased within the previous 30 days
* At Risk — last purchase was 31–60 days ago
* Inactive — last purchase was more than 60 days ago

This framework can help identify customers who may benefit from re-engagement campaigns.

## Recommendations

1. Monitor Electronics performance because it generated the highest revenue in the dataset.
2. Investigate lower-revenue products to understand potential sales opportunities.
3. Monitor high-value customers and encourage repeat purchases.
4. Use customer activity status to identify customers who may need re-engagement.
5. Continue collecting sales data so that trends can be analyzed over a longer period.

## Limitations

This project uses a small fictional dataset created for portfolio and learning purposes.

The analysis demonstrates SQL techniques and business analysis methods but should not be treated as evidence of real-world customer or sales behavior.

A larger real-world dataset would be required before making operational decisions.
